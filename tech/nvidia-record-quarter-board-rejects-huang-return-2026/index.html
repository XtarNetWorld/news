Nvidia Made $115 Billion Without Jensen Huang — And Its Board Just Told Him He's Not Coming Back

Nvidia's revenue more than doubled to a record $115.8 billion in its first earnings report since Jensen Huang resigned — and on the same call, the board said it has no intention of bringing him back.

MS By Monu Sharma
• November 19, 2026 • 5 min read • Tech

Nvidia has just posted the biggest quarter in its history — $115.8 billion of revenue in three months, more than double a year ago — without the man who built the company. On the same earnings call, the board made a second announcement almost as loud as the numbers: Jensen Huang, who resigned last week and then offered to come back, is not coming back.

The permanent CEO search — and whether Kress gets the job herself.

The numbers first

For the third quarter of fiscal 2027, the three months ended 25 October, Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier and 20% from the previous quarter — well above its own $108 billion forecast. Data Center delivered $107.6 billion, up 110% year on year, about 93% of everything Nvidia sold. Net income reached $65.3 billion, or $2.70 per diluted share, against $31.9 billion and $1.30 a year ago.

The number that explains the quarter is Rubin. Vera Rubin NVL72, the rack-scale platform that follows Blackwell, began shipping during the quarter — the fastest product ramp in Nvidia history. It produced nearly $20 billion in its debut quarter, with racks already running at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure and Nebius.

Guidance was stronger still: about $130 billion this quarter, plus or minus 3%, in a 14-week quarter, above the roughly $125 billion analysts expected. Nvidia also raised its fiscal 2028 revenue growth target from about 70% to about 75%, assuming no data center revenue from China at all.

The board’s answer was no

The earnings call on Tuesday 17 November was the first without Huang leading it since he co-founded Nvidia in 1993. Board chair Harvey Jones opened by settling the week’s big question: the board, he said, “unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.”

Hours earlier, Huang had broken his silence with an apologetic statement, saying he would be honoured to return if the board felt it served the company’s best interests. The board’s answer came the same day: no. It has hired Heidrick & Struggles to find a permanent chief executive, and Wachtell Lipton to lead an independent inquiry into Huang’s conduct.

Huang resigned on 12 November, days after the board opened its probe. The reports that led to his departure alleged extramarital affairs, including one with a woman US officials concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset. That allegation remains attributed and unverified: Reuters has said it could not independently verify the claims, and China’s foreign ministry called the “spy narrative” unsubstantiated. Nothing available establishes that secrets were passed or compromised. Huang has admitted infidelity and apologised, but that is not an admission of passing secrets.

Kress changes the tone

If the call had a second story, it was Colette Kress. Nvidia’s chief financial officer since 2013, now interim chief executive, ran the earnings call — and used it to change the company’s tone. Where Huang sold the future in the biggest possible numbers, Kress took numbers away.

The clearest example is OpenAI. In the second quarter, Nvidia had talked up its $105 billion lease guarantee for OpenAI’s Ohio campus, saying each chip generation there could generate $150 billion to $200 billion. Kress stripped that $200 billion pitch from the report, replacing it with a building-by-building schedule for how the guarantee takes effect across nine buildings from fiscal 2029 — and noting Nvidia has not yet paid out any of its guarantees.

At the same time she gave shareholders something concrete: a new $150 billion buyback authorisation, and higher supply and memory commitments — $279 billion to $390 billion — against the memory shortages constraining the AI build-out. Shares rose in after-hours trading on Tuesday, then faded the next morning. The stock is down about 24% this month.

The China shadow

The same week, the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission said in its annual report that China’s military is seeking advanced Nvidia chips for AI-enabled weapons, trying to circumvent US export controls, because domestic processors from Huawei and Cambricon still lag behind Nvidia and AMD.

Nvidia’s answer has been to walk away from China entirely. Kress disclosed $2.4 billion of licensed Hopper chip sales to China last quarter — and that Nvidia has now stopped shipping those products there. Fourth-quarter guidance assumes zero data center revenue from China. Huang is due before congressional committees on 1 December, followed by general counsel Tim Teter on 2 December.

What happens next

Three things will decide what Nvidia looks like in six months. The chief executive search: an insider, an outsider, or Kress herself. The Wachtell Lipton inquiry and the December hearings, which will test the board’s “no reason to believe” position on secrets. And Rubin: whether a platform that did nearly $20 billion in its first quarter keeps ramping, and whether the 75% growth target for fiscal 2028 survives memory supply, margins — gross margin is guided down to 71–72% next quarter — and a world where Nvidia no longer sells its best chips to China.

The quarter proved Nvidia’s business does not depend on one charismatic founder to sell chips. The board’s decision proved it does not intend to depend on him again. Both facts are on the record, in the same week, in the same call.

Quick Answers
How much money did Nvidia make last quarter? +

Revenue of $115.8 billion for the third quarter of fiscal 2027, up 103% year on year and 20% on the previous quarter. Data Center revenue was $107.6 billion, up 110%, and net income was $65.3 billion, or $2.70 per diluted share.

Is Jensen Huang coming back as Nvidia CEO? +

No. Board chair Harvey Jones said on the 17 November earnings call that the board unanimously accepted Huang’s resignation and “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.” Colette Kress is interim CEO while a permanent search, run by Heidrick & Struggles, is under way.

What did Nvidia say about China? +

It has stopped shipping licensed Hopper chips to China after $2.4 billion of such sales last quarter, and its fourth-quarter guidance assumes no data center compute revenue from China.
