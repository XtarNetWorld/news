Nvidia's Board Is Investigating Its Own CEO — and the Shares Fell 4.5% Before the Market Opened

Nvidia said on Sunday 8 November it is opening an independent investigation into whether founder and CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The board says it has no reason to believe secrets were compromised — but the market moved before it could say more.

MS By Monu Sharma
• November 9, 2026 • 5 min read • Tech
01 The probe

A committee of independent directors, with outside counsel, will investigate whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort. Huang will cooperate fully.

02 The claim

Reports allege a relationship with a woman US officials concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset. Reuters could not independently verify the claims.

03 The market

Nvidia fell ~4.5% premarket Monday; futures were ~$180.30 vs Friday’s Nasdaq close of $186.64 (−3.4%).

The most valuable company in the world spent its Sunday investigating its own chief executive. Nvidia, the chipmaker at the centre of the artificial-intelligence boom, said on 8 November that its board is initiating an independent investigation into whether founder and CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. By Monday morning, before the US market had even opened, the shares had fallen about 4.5% in premarket trading. What follows is what is known, what is alleged and not verified, and what the market and Washington have said so far — kept strictly separate, because in this story the difference between the three is the story.

What Nvidia announced on Sunday

Nvidia said on Sunday 8 November that it is initiating an independent investigation, run by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel, into whether Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The company said Huang will cooperate fully. The board added that it has “no reason to believe” US national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised — a statement of its current position, not a finding.

The wording matters. A board investigating its own chief executive is rare at any company; at the world’s most valuable one, in the middle of an AI investment boom, it is extraordinary. The structure Nvidia chose — independent directors, outside counsel — is the standard way a board tries to show the process is not being run by the person under scrutiny.

What is alleged — and what is not verified

The investigation follows reporting by the New York Times in late October and the Financial Times on 6 November. The Times reported that Huang had a relationship with Alice Huang, a Chinese citizen and former executive of Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer, whom US officials concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset who passed information to Beijing. Megaspeed is separately under US investigation over suspected help getting barred Nvidia AI chips to China.

None of that is established fact. Reuters, reporting the board’s announcement, stated explicitly that it could not independently verify these claims, nor Alice Huang’s current employment or location. China’s foreign ministry called the “spy narrative” unsubstantiated. This article repeats the allegation only as an attributed report, and does not present as fact that Huang passed secrets to Beijing, that Alice Huang is a spy, or that any secrets were compromised — because none of those things has been verified by the sources available.

The admission in Huang’s message to employees

On Sunday, Huang told employees he had been unfaithful to his wife and apologised, and committed to winning back the confidence of the board, partners and shareholders. That admission is on the record from Huang himself, via Reuters’ reporting of his message. It is separate from the intelligence allegation: an admission of infidelity is not an admission of passing secrets, and nothing in the reporting available establishes such a link.

The market’s verdict, before the bell

Nvidia fell about 4.5% in premarket trading on Monday. After-hours and perpetual-futures trading had the shares at about $180.30, against Friday’s Nasdaq close of $186.64 — a fall of about 3.4% from the close. Intel, Marvell and Qualcomm were also down 0.5%+ in premarket trading. The wider market, by contrast, rose: Dow e-minis were up 0.73%, the S&P 500 up 0.40% and the Nasdaq-100 up 0.21% at 05:10 ET.

Two cautions belong with those numbers. First, premarket and futures prices are not the day’s close: the US session had not opened at the time of reporting, so there is no Monday close to quote. Second, a 4.5% premarket fall in a company valued at $4.5 trillion as of 6 November is a very large amount of value in absolute terms. The next hard number comes on 17 November, when Nvidia reports earnings.

Washington, Seoul — and the calendar

President Donald Trump said on Sunday that Huang was “honorable” and “a fine person”, that he had not spoken to him about the matter, and that he would take Huang on his Asia trip next week. Senior House Republicans have sought briefings from Huang on national-security grounds, following the Times report. Neither the praise nor the request for briefings is a finding about what happened; both show how quickly a corporate governance matter at Nvidia becomes a national-security conversation in Washington, because Nvidia’s chips sit at the centre of the US–China technology contest.

In Seoul, South Korea said contracted GPU supplies will not be disrupted — 10,000 GPUs already delivered under a 2025 budget, with 2026 units under contract — and Nvidia’s AI Day in Seoul proceeded as planned.

What happens next

Three things will decide how this story develops: what the committee reports and when; whether Monday’s premarket fall holds once the US session opens; and whether the House briefings change the board’s “no reason to believe” position.

Until one of those moves, the honest summary is short. The board is investigating whether its chief executive was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The allegation is unverified. The chief executive has admitted infidelity and apologised. The shares fell before the market opened.

Quick Answers
What is Nvidia investigating? +

Whether founder and CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside counsel, will run the investigation, and Huang will cooperate fully.

Is it proven that secrets were passed to Beijing? +

No. The allegation comes from press reports; Reuters states it could not independently verify the claims, and China’s foreign ministry called the “spy narrative” unsubstantiated. Nvidia’s board says it has no reason to believe secrets were compromised.

How did the shares react? +

Nvidia fell about 4.5% in premarket trading on Monday, with futures at about $180.30 vs Friday’s Nasdaq close of $186.64. No Monday US close existed at the time of reporting.

When is Nvidia’s next earnings report? +

17 November 2026. Nvidia’s valuation was $4.5 trillion as of 6 November, according to Reuters.
