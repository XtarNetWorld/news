Today Only: The Taj Mahal, Red Fort and 141 Other Monuments Are Free — Here’s the One Catch

The Archaeological Survey of India has waived entry fees at all its protected monuments across the country on Thursday 19 November 2026, the opening day of World Heritage Week. The Taj Mahal, Agra Fort, Red Fort and Qutub Minar are all free to enter — but one fee still applies.

MS By Monu Sharma
• November 19, 2026 • 5 min read • Culture
01 The day

Free entry on Thursday 19 November 2026 only, the opening day of World Heritage Week (19–25 November).

02 The scale

All ASI-protected monuments — more than 3,800 sites, of which 143 are ticketed, including the Taj Mahal, Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri, Red Fort and Qutub Minar.

03 The catch

The ₹200 fee to enter the Taj Mahal’s main mausoleum still applies. General entry to the complex is free.

04 The who

The waiver covers domestic and foreign visitors alike, at every ASI monument in the country.

There is a day every year when the most famous building in India costs nothing to walk into. That day is today. The Taj Mahal, the Red Fort, the Qutub Minar and 140 other ticketed monuments are free to enter — but before you plan the trip, know the one fee that has not been waived.

What is free today

Entry to all centrally protected monuments and archaeological sites across the country will be free on Thursday to mark the beginning of World Heritage Week, the Archaeological Survey of India has said. World Heritage Week is observed every year from 19 to 25 November.

The ASI protects more than 3,800 monuments and sites across India, of which 143 are ticketed. The waiver covers all of them — the Taj Mahal, Agra Fort and Fatehpur Sikri in Agra, and the Red Fort and Qutub Minar in Delhi among them — for domestic and foreign tourists alike. The Culture Ministry’s announcement on X put it simply: mark World Heritage Week with free entry to all ASI monuments.

The one fee that still applies

There is a catch, and it is a small one. Visitors to the Taj Mahal will still have to pay the prescribed ₹200 fee to access the main mausoleum — the domed chamber itself — ASI Superintending Archaeologist Smita Kumar said. The general entry to the Taj complex, the gardens and the reflecting pool is free; the mausoleum is not.

That distinction matters if you are travelling to Agra specifically for the Taj. The free day covers the ticket you would normally buy at the gate. The mausoleum ticket is a separate, additional charge on any day, and today is no exception.

Why the ASI does this

The waiver is the opening gesture of a week meant to get people to look at the monuments they walk past. Exhibitions will be held during the week and competitions organised for schoolchildren, to raise awareness about heritage conservation — the point of World Heritage Week in the first place.

The official quoted by the agencies was careful on one point: the waiver applies only on 19 November for now, with no announcement of an extension for the remaining days of the week. If the free day is the reason to go, today is the day.

Quick Answers
Which monuments are free on 19 November 2026? +

All ASI-protected monuments across India, including the Taj Mahal, Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri, Red Fort and Qutub Minar. The ASI protects over 3,800 sites, of which 143 are ticketed.

Is the Taj Mahal completely free today? +

General entry to the Taj Mahal complex is free on 19 November. The ₹200 fee to enter the main mausoleum still applies.

Does free entry continue through World Heritage Week? +

No announcement has been made of an extension. The waiver applies only on 19 November, the opening day of the week (19–25 November).
