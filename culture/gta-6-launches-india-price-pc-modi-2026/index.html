GTA 6 Is Finally Here — ₹5,999, 99 on Metacritic, and Modi's Growth Story

After 13 years, Grand Theft Auto VI unlocked in India at midnight on 19 November 2026 — on consoles only, at ₹5,999, with a 99 on Metacritic and an estimated $1.5 billion already banked in pre-orders. Then the Prime Minister joined in.

MS By Monu Sharma
• November 19, 2026 • 5 min read • Culture
01 The wait

GTA VI unlocked in India at midnight, 19 November 2026, ending a 13-year wait since GTA 5 in September 2013.

02 The price

₹5,999 Standard, ₹7,499 Ultimate, on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S only. No PC version or date announced.

03 The score

99 on Metacritic from around 150 critics, joint-highest ever, matching Zelda: Ocarina of Time. IGN and GameSpot both gave it 10/10.

04 The money

An estimated nearly 16 million digital pre-orders worth over $1.5 billion before launch, per Sensor Tower — not an official Rockstar or Take-Two figure.

Thirteen years is a long time to wait for anything. At midnight on Thursday, 19 November 2026, the wait ended in India: Grand Theft Auto VI unlocked on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. The reviews say it was worth it. The price says it will not be cheap. And by Thursday morning, the Prime Minister had turned it into a story about India’s growth.

The midnight unlock

GTA VI unlocked in India at 12 am IST on 19 November, hours after New Zealand got it first. Players who had pre-ordered and pre-loaded could start the moment the clock turned — though the download was the real gatekeeper. The game needs around 212GB of space, a file so large that on a 100Mbps connection it takes close to five hours to pull down.

The setting is Leonida, Rockstar’s fictionalised Florida, with Vice City — its version of Miami — at the centre. The story follows Lucia Caminos and Jason Duval, a criminal duo caught in a conspiracy bigger than either of them. It is the first GTA with a woman as a lead, and the first to return to Vice City since 2002.

What it costs to play in India

In India the Standard Edition costs ₹5,999 and the Ultimate Edition ₹7,499, on both the PlayStation Store and the Xbox Store — roughly double what GTA 5 cost here at launch. And the game is only the first bill: a PS5 Slim lists at around ₹44,990 and an Xbox Series S at around ₹31,990.

There is one more catch, and it is the one PC players already know: there is no PC version, and Rockstar has announced no PC date at all. The launch is consoles only. PC players, a large share of India’s gaming audience, are left waiting with no timeline.

There is no disc: Rockstar is selling the game as a code in a box, and Blinkit turned that into a launch-day event, delivering a collectible box with a download code inside to select areas of 11 cities — Delhi, Gurugram, Faridabad, Ghaziabad, Noida, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Mumbai, Pune, Kolkata and Ahmedabad — from 18 November. Co-founder Albinder Dhindsa announced it on X: it took 13 years for GTA 6 to arrive, and Blinkit would get it to you in minutes.

One technical limit is worth knowing before you buy: the game is capped at 30 frames per second on all consoles, with no 60fps mode even on the PS5 Pro. For a game this expensive, that cap will be the first argument in every review thread — and no settings menu can fix it.

The reviews: 99 out of 100

If the price is the hard part, the reviews are the easy part. GTA VI holds a 99 on Metacritic from around 150 critics — joint-highest ever on the site, matching The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, a record that has stood since 1998. Not one review in the count was mixed or negative, and IGN and GameSpot both awarded it a perfect 10/10. Critics have called it a masterpiece.

The money before a single copy was played

The most startling number in this launch is not the review score. It is the money that changed hands before anyone could play. Sensor Tower, which tracks digital store sales, estimates GTA VI had nearly 16 million digital pre-orders — 15.88 million in its latest tracker reading — worth over $1.5 billion before launch day. Around three-quarters were for the PlayStation 5.

Keep the label attached: it is an estimate, not an official number. Rockstar and its parent company Take-Two have published no pre-order total, and a pre-order can still be cancelled. But the market has made its own judgement: Take-Two shares climbed roughly 6–7% on Wednesday 18 November, the day the reviews landed.

The day India took off — and the Prime Minister joined in

Launch day in India looked less like a product release and more like a public holiday nobody had declared. The HR platform Remote reported a 32% jump in leave requests worldwide for 19 November, and in India the midnight unlock was marked with memes and fireworks.

Then the Prime Minister joined it. On Thursday morning, Narendra Modi posted a GTA-inspired “then vs now” carousel on X, captioned “India is playing a whole new game!” — a 10-photo carousel covering nine sectors, from the economy and poverty to sanitation, renewables, space, rail electrification, digital connectivity, defence exports and Naxalism.

Not everyone applauded the framing. Congress leader Jairam Ramesh criticised the GTA comparison, arguing the Prime Minister was reaching out to young voters through a game that follows a criminal duo. Within hours, a launch-day meme had become a political argument.

What happens next

Three things will decide how this week is remembered: whether the pre-order estimates become the biggest entertainment launch in history once official numbers arrive; whether the 99 holds; and, for Indian players, when — or whether — a PC version is coming. Until Rockstar says so, nobody knows. For tonight, the wait is over for console players: 13 years, ₹5,999, 212GB, 99 out of 100 — and a Prime Minister who decided the game was about India all along.
