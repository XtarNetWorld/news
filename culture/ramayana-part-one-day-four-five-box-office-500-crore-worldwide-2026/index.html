Ramayana Crosses ₹500 Crore Worldwide in Four Days — and Monday Is Loading Bigger

On Diwali day itself, Ramayana Part One collected ₹56.15 crore in India — down from Saturday’s ₹69.80 crore, but enough to take the four-day worldwide total past ₹500 crore. And Monday’s live tracking was already near ₹50 crore by mid-afternoon.

Four days after release, Ramayana Part One has done what its advance bookings promised and its first weekend confirmed: it is now a ₹500-crore film worldwide. Sunday, Diwali day itself, brought ₹56.15 crore in India — lower than Saturday’s ₹69.80 crore, which is the normal shape of a holiday weekend, and still enough to push the four-day worldwide gross to ₹507.20 crore, according to trade tracker Sacnilk via CNBC TV18. The more interesting number, though, is the one still moving as this article is published: Monday’s live collection, which had already reached ₹49.69 crore by 3:03 pm. Diwali day was the celebration. Monday is the test of whether the celebration becomes a run.

Day 4: Sunday, Diwali day

Ramayana Part One collected ₹56.15 crore net in India on Sunday 8 November, its fourth day in theatres and Diwali day itself, across 21,770 shows, according to Sacnilk via CNBC TV18 and the Times of India. That is 19.6% lower than Saturday’s ₹69.80 crore — the film’s biggest day so far — which is the normal pattern for a festival weekend: Saturday is the peak, Sunday dips as families spend the day at home for the festival itself.

The dip should not be misread as a stumble. A ₹56.15-crore Sunday is, by any recent standard, an enormous day — it broke the single-day Diwali box-office record previously held by Tiger 3 (₹44.50 crore in 2023), per Outlook India. About 23.53 lakh tickets were sold on Sunday alone.

The four-day table

The day-wise run, all India net, per Sacnilk: Day 1 (Thursday) ₹64.85 crore, Day 2 (Friday) ₹48.05 crore, Day 3 (Saturday) ₹69.80 crore, Day 4 (Sunday) ₹56.15 crore. Four-day India net: ₹238.85 crore. India gross: ₹285.20 crore. Overseas: ₹222 crore, after ₹53 crore on Day 4 alone. Worldwide gross: ₹507.20 crore.

That crossed two milestones at once: in India, the film passed ₹200 crore net — and passed the four-day domestic total of Prabhas’s Adipurush (₹237 crore), according to Outlook India. Worldwide, it crossed ₹500 crore in four days.

The ₹500 crore question: whose total?

Not every outlet agrees on the worldwide figure, and the disagreement is worth explaining rather than hiding. Sacnilk, via CNBC TV18, puts the four-day worldwide gross at ₹507.20 crore. The Financial Express, NDTV Profit and Times Now put it at ₹499.20 crore. The difference is Day 4 overseas: ₹53 crore in Sacnilk’s count against ₹45 crore in the other set. This article uses Sacnilk’s set consistently — the same tracker for every day — and describes the total as approximately ₹500 crore, because the ₹8-crore gap is a tracker difference on overseas estimates, not a dispute about the Indian collections, which all sources agree on.

Day 5 is live — and Monday is loading bigger

Monday 9 November is the day the trade has been waiting for. Day 5 advance booking beat opening day: ₹50.03 crore in advance sales against ₹42.96 crore of opening-day pre-sales, according to NDTV Profit, with about 13 lakh tickets booked in advance. BookMyShow saw over a lakh tickets booked in a single hour — the precise hourly figure differs between reports (1.40 lakh per TezzBuzz, over 1.10 lakh per Sacnilk’s headline), so this article states only the figure both agree on: over a lakh in an hour.

The live tracker at 3:03 pm IST on Monday showed Day 5 at ₹49.69 crore net — Hindi ₹41.41 crore, Kannada ₹3.72 crore, Telugu ₹2.53 crore, Tamil ₹2.03 crore — across 12,591 shows, taking the five-day India net to ₹288.54 crore and India gross to ₹343.83 crore. The Times of India live blog showed ₹44.22 crore at about 3:17 pm — the same live tracker at an earlier snapshot. Live figures move through the day; the final Day 5 figure lands tonight.

One projection is circulating that should be labelled as what it is: Sacnilk and ETV Bharat have floated the possibility of a ₹100-crore gross single day. That is a projection, not a result, and it depends on evening shows.

The records around the numbers

Context helps place the run. Over the 5–8 November weekend, Godzilla Minus Zero was the world’s No. 1 film and Ramayana No. 2 globally, per a Sacnilk headline. In India, the film is now among the top five Bollywood earners of 2026 after four days, per The Week, with a Hindi share above 80% of the India total.

What to watch tonight

Three numbers matter tonight. The final Day 5 net — whether it clears ₹60 crore, ₹70 crore, or Saturday’s ₹69.80 crore. The five-day India net, which at 3:03 pm already stood at ₹288.54 crore, putting ₹300 crore within reach on Day 5 itself. And the overseas update, which will decide whether the worldwide total is quoted as ₹550 crore or higher by tomorrow morning.

For now, the position is simple. Four days, ₹238.85 crore in India, about ₹500 crore worldwide, a Diwali-day record, and a Monday already at ₹49.69 crore by mid-afternoon. The next milestone is one no advance booking can guarantee: a Monday bigger than its Saturday.
