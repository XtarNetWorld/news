Sensex Has Its Best Week in Six Years: Up 8.82% as Foreign Money Pours Back While the Nasdaq Slides

The Sensex jumped 2.17% on Friday and 8.82% for the week — its best week in about six years — as foreign investors net-bought Rs 45,960 crore. The same week, the Nasdaq fell 3.4%, its fourth straight weekly decline. Is India really the safe harbour from the AI panic — or just the next crowded trade?

MS By Monu Sharma
• November 7, 2026 • 5 min read • Business
01 Friday

Sensex closed at 81,028.02, up 1,718.01 (+2.17%); Nifty at 25,030.35, up 493.75 (+2.01%) — the 7th straight winning session.

02 The week

Sensex +8.82% (+6,568.49 points), Nifty +8.58% (+1,977.25 points) — the biggest weekly gain in about six years.

03 Foreign money

Foreign investors net-bought Rs 45,960 crore in the week of 2–6 November, a record weekly figure.

04 Nasdaq

On Thursday, 5 November, the Nasdaq had its worst day in 19 months. It closed at 24,315.00, down 5.52% — more than 10% below its 6 October record, which by the market’s own definition puts it in a correction. Nvidia alone lost more than $440 billion in a day, falling 9.3%. On Friday, Dalal Street did the opposite.

Next week’s foreign-investor flows — trend, or one week of rebalancing?

Friday, and the week, in numbers

On Friday, 6 November, the Sensex closed at 81,028.02, up 1,718.01 points, or 2.17%. The Nifty 50 closed at 25,030.35, up 493.75 points, or 2.01%. It was the seventh straight winning session for both indices. For the week, the Sensex gained 6,568.49 points — 8.82% — and the Nifty gained 1,977.25 points — 8.58%. Those are the biggest weekly gains for both indices in about six years, since February 2021.

An earlier partial-week figure — about 7% and 5,336 points for the Sensex — should not be used; these are the full-week figures for 2–6 November. Foreign investors, heavy sellers in October, net-bought Rs 45,960 crore of Indian shares during the full week, a record weekly figure. An earlier figure of Rs 31,786.57 crore, reported by The Week, covered only part of the week — it excludes Friday — and should not be presented as the full-week figure.

The contrast: Wall Street bled, Dalal Street rallied

The contrast is the story. The same fear that crushed American chip stocks — that the AI buildout is overvalued, over-regulated and over-leveraged — is pushing money toward a market that has very little exposure to AI hardware at all. India does not make the chips; it sells the services that run on them. When investors started asking whether the AI trade had gone too far, they treated India as the place that was not part of it.

The Nasdaq’s week tells the other half. Thursday’s close of 24,315.00, down 5.52%, was the verified figure — not 24,712.60, down 3.9%, as NewsXphere’s own 6 November article wrongly reported. On Friday the Nasdaq partly rebounded, closing at 24,579.42, up 1.09% (264.43 points). For the week, the Nasdaq still fell 3.4% — its fourth straight weekly decline, confirmed. The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,376.86, up 0.31%, and the Dow at 51,285.73, down 0.46%.

What drove Friday: earnings, not AI

Bharti Airtel was one of Friday’s drivers. Its second-quarter revenue rose 16.7% year-on-year to Rs 60,878 crore, and net profit rose 30.9%. Mahindra & Mahindra surged about 5% on strong second-quarter results of its own. These are not AI stocks. They are a telecom company and a tractor-and-SUV maker. Their strength on a day when the world’s most valuable technology companies were falling is the point: the money rotating out of American AI is not looking for the next AI stock. It is looking for earnings that have nothing to do with AI.

There is a number doing the rounds that deserves care. JPMorgan has estimated that if foreign long-only investors move from underweight to neutral on India, about $115 billion in net inflows could follow. That is a scenario estimate, not a forecast — it describes what would happen if a particular shift occurred, not what will happen. It is also a different $115 billion from the “AI’s Bill” chip-deals story earlier this week — the two have nothing to do with each other.

The case against the celebration

And there is a case against the celebration, which should be heard before anyone calls India a safe harbour. Some analysts quoted in the coverage question the whole thesis, pointing out that Indian shares trade at about 18.6 times forward earnings, against about 9.3 times for China. India is not cheap. If the money coming in is chasing safety, it is buying safety at a premium price.

“Safe harbour” is a thesis, not a fact. A safe harbour is a claim about the future, not a description of one week. That does not prove India is safe; it proves India had a very good week on real earnings and returning foreign money. What happens next depends on two things: whether the American correction deepens or stabilises, and whether Indian earnings keep justifying the prices being paid for them. Airtel and Mahindra did their part this week. The test is whether the rest of the market does the same.

What to watch next

For now, the scoreboard is simple. Wall Street’s AI stocks lost trillions in a week. Dalal Street gained 8.82%. The question is whether it means something, or whether it is just the week the money changed its mind.

Foreign investors sold heavily in October and bought heavily this week. That kind of reversal can be the start of a new trend, or it can be a single week of rebalancing that reverses again just as fast. The difference will show up in next week’s flows, not in this week’s headlines. Until then, the honest description of India’s week is the modest one: a very good week, for real reasons, in a market that is expensive and knows it. This is not investment advice.
