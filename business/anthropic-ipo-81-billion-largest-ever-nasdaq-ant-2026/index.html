Anthropic Just Pulled Off the Biggest IPO in History — $81.3 Billion

Anthropic priced its shares at $83 on Wednesday 18 November, raising $81.3 billion in the largest IPO ever at pricing. It starts trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on Thursday — and the AI lab behind Claude is now worth about $1.5 trillion.

MS By Monu Sharma
• November 19, 2026 • 5 min read • Business
01 The price

Priced at $83 a share on Wednesday 18 November — above the $70–$78 range — selling 980 million shares, upsized from 825 million.

02 The raise

$81.3 billion raised — the largest IPO ever at pricing, ahead of SpaceX’s $75 billion June listing. A concurrent $26 billion private placement takes the total to $107.3 billion.

03 The value

About $1.5 trillion fully diluted — roughly the world’s 12th-biggest company, just behind Tesla.

04 The debut

Starts trading on the Nasdaq as ANT on Thursday 19 November, led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley with a 22-bank syndicate.

Five years ago, Anthropic was a small lab founded by researchers who had walked out of OpenAI. On Wednesday night it priced the biggest initial public offering in history. On Thursday morning, anyone with a brokerage account can buy a piece of the company behind Claude — at a valuation of about $1.5 trillion.

The biggest listing ever — with one footnote

Anthropic and its seven co-founders sold about 980 million shares at $83 each — 956.4 million from the company and 23.6 million from the founders — taking gross proceeds to $81.3 billion, CNBC reported. It is the largest IPO ever at pricing, comfortably ahead of SpaceX’s $75 billion initial raise in June.

There is one footnote worth keeping. SpaceX’s total rose to $85.7 billion once underwriters exercised their overallotment option, according to the Economic Times. Anthropic clears even that bar once you count the $26 billion private placement being raised alongside the IPO at the same $83 price — $107.3 billion of capital in one go.

The demand was the story of the pricing. The deal was upsized by about 19% from 825 million shares, and priced above a range that had already been set high. Five years ago, Anthropic raised $124 million from its first investors. On Wednesday it raised roughly 650 times that in a single night.

Why investors paid up

Anthropic is the company behind Claude, the AI assistant that has become the default coding and reasoning tool for much of the tech industry. It was founded in 2021 by former OpenAI researchers, and it is the first major frontier AI lab to list its shares.

The numbers in its filing explain the price. Its annualised revenue run rate reached $81 billion as of 31 October — up $10 billion in a single month from 30 September. For the nine months ended 30 September, it reported revenue of $33.24 billion, up roughly 1,200% from the same period last year, according to its prospectus.

The other side of that growth is the bill. Anthropic spent about $9 billion on computing power in the first half of 2026 alone. The IPO money is, in large part, money to keep buying the chips and data-centre capacity that the models run on.

What $1.5 trillion actually means

On a fully diluted basis — counting stock options and restricted stock — the $83 price values Anthropic at about $1.5 trillion. That makes it roughly the world’s 12th-biggest company, just behind Tesla, according to Al Jazeera’s tally.

Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley led the offering, with 20 other banks in the syndicate. The listing also sets the marker for the rest of the AI industry: OpenAI, the company Anthropic’s founders left, is expected to follow with its own IPO in 2027.

What to watch on day one

Three things decide whether Thursday is a triumph or a warning. First, the opening trade: does ANT open above $83, and does it stay there? Second, whether the AI-stock wobble of recent weeks — investors asking whether the trillions flowing into AI will ever pay off — shows up in the price. And third, whether Anthropic can turn an $81 billion revenue run rate into actual profit while spending $9 billion a half-year on compute.

For now, the record is the story: the biggest IPO ever, from a company that did not exist six years ago, selling the technology that every other big company is now racing to own.
