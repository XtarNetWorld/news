70 for 5 to 243 for 5: How New Zealand Stole Day One — and Why Siraj and Jadeja’s Absence Is the Story

New Zealand finished Day 1 of the first Test at Wellington on 243 for 5 — after being 70 for 5. Henry Nicholls (113 not out) and Glenn Phillips (94 not out) put on an unbroken 173. India left out Mohammed Siraj and Ravindra Jadeja, and by stumps the decision was the biggest talking point in Indian cricket.

MS By Monu Sharma
• November 19, 2026 • 5 min read • World
01 The score

New Zealand 243/5 at stumps, 82 overs, after being 70/5. Nicholls 113* (221 balls), Phillips 94* (182 balls).

02 The stand

An unbroken 173-run sixth-wicket partnership — Nicholls’s fifth Test century at the Basin Reserve.

03 The bowling

Bumrah 3/52 was the pick; Kamboj 1/57, Akash Deep 1/33, Prasidh 0/58, Sundar 0/38.

04 The selection

India dropped Mohammed Siraj and Ravindra Jadeja; four seamers plus Washington Sundar played instead. Both seniors were in the XI for the last Test, against Sri Lanka in August.

At lunch, it looked like a demolition. Jasprit Bumrah had New Zealand reeling at 70 for 5 on a blustery Wellington morning, and India had the first Test in their pocket. By stumps, the same two batsmen were still there, the scoreboard read 243 for 5, and the only question anyone was asking was about the two men India had left out.

Day 2: can India break the stand early — or do Nicholls and Phillips bat India out of the Test?

The morning belonged to Bumrah

Shubman Gill won the toss and chose to bowl first on a blustery, cold morning at the Basin Reserve, naming four seamers — Jasprit Bumrah, Prasidh Krishna, Akash Deep and Anshul Kamboj — with Washington Sundar as the only spin-bowling all-rounder. There was no place for Mohammed Siraj, and none for Ravindra Jadeja.

For a session, the call looked inspired. Bumrah’s mastery had the hosts shaken: Latham fell for a duck, Conway went, Rachin Ravindra gifted his wicket, Daryl Mitchell managed 1, and after lunch Bumrah removed Tom Blundell. New Zealand were 70 for 5, and India looked on course to bowl them out on the opening day itself.

The rescue: 173 runs, no wicket

Then Henry Nicholls and Glenn Phillips happened. Nicholls, who loves batting at the Basin Reserve, struck a majestic unbeaten 113 off 221 balls, with 14 fours — his fifth Test century at the venue, arriving at the crease off the third ball of the innings and batting almost the entire day. Phillips, tenacious for two sessions, finished on 94 not out off 182 balls, with 13 fours.

Their unbroken 173-run stand for the sixth wicket turned Bumrah’s 3 for 52 into a footnote. The day ended in further ignominy for India when Devdutt Padikkal dropped Nicholls in the last hour, after a mix-up with Rishabh Pant. New Zealand took stumps at 243 for 5 in 82 overs, with both batsmen still there.

The rest of the attack could not back Bumrah up. Kamboj, the surprise pick ahead of Siraj, took 1 for 57; Akash Deep 1 for 33; Prasidh Krishna went wicketless for 58; Sundar 0 for 38. Once the sun dried the moisture out of the surface, the conditions turned — and India had no answer.

The selection row that will not go away

The omission of Siraj and Jadeja was the story before a ball was bowled, and it is a bigger story now. Both were in India’s most recent Test, against Sri Lanka in August, and both played vital roles in the 2025 England tour that India drew 2–2. Sunil Gavaskar questioned Siraj’s omission, saying the pacer has consistently delivered on the big stage; former all-rounder Ravichandran Ashwin questioned how the XI was arrived at, aiming his criticism at captain Gill, and said being dropped suddenly could dent both players’ confidence.

Batting coach Sitanshu Kotak defended the calls after play: there was nothing wrong with Siraj, he said, and the team felt this was the best XI for the conditions; Sundar was preferred over Jadeja because New Zealand’s top order had four left-handers. “We don’t pick a team only for day one, but also for days two, three and four,” he said. Siraj, for his part, spent the day as a substitute fielder, trying to fire up the bowlers.

The stakes are real. India, fourth in the World Test Championship standings behind New Zealand, need to win both Tests in the series to keep their hopes of qualifying for the final alive. Day 2 starts with India needing a wicket — and with the selection calls likely to remain the talking point whatever happens.
