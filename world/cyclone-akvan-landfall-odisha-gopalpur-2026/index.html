1 Lakh Moved to Safety as Cyclone Akvan Races Toward Odisha — Landfall at Noon Tomorrow, 110 kmph Winds

Severe cyclonic storm Akvan is moving north across the Bay of Bengal and will cross the coast near Gopalpur, between Narsapur and Puri, by noon on Friday 20 November, with sustained winds of 90–100 kmph gusting to 110. Odisha has already shifted 1,05,304 people to 1,224 shelters — and the count is still rising.

There are about eighteen hours between Odisha and the storm. Severe cyclonic storm Akvan is moving north across the Bay of Bengal and will cross the coast near Gopalpur — between Narsapur in Andhra Pradesh and Puri in Odisha — by noon on Friday 20 November, the IMD says, with sustained winds of 90–100 kmph gusting to 110. By Thursday afternoon, Odisha had already moved 1,05,304 people into 1,224 shelters. The state’s target is zero casualties, and it wants two lakh people out of harm’s way before the sea comes ashore.

Friday noon near Gopalpur — the crossing, then first damage and rainfall reports from Ganjam, Puri and Gajapati.

Where Akvan is now, and when it lands

At 5:30 am on Thursday 19 November, Akvan lay about 440 km east-southeast of Narsapur, 530 km south of Gopalpur and 590 km south of Puri, after moving north at 12 kmph in the previous six hours, per the IMD’s morning bulletin. A later bulletin had it closer — 420 km east-southeast of Narsapur, 490 km south of Gopalpur — moving north at 15 kmph. Direction all day: north, then north-northwest, toward the Odisha coast.

The landfall forecast has also steadied after a day of uncertainty about the track. The IMD now says Akvan will cross the north Andhra Pradesh and Yanam and south Odisha coasts between Narsapur and Puri, close to east of Gopalpur, by noon of Friday 20 November, with maximum sustained winds of 90–100 kmph gusting to 110 kmph. Amaravati experts note a slight chance it lands near the extreme end of north coastal Andhra instead — hence the corridor, Narsapur to Puri, not a single point. (The IMD spells it “Akvan”, and calls it “severe” — not “very severe”, a different category on its scale.)

One lakh people, moved in a day

The evacuation is the story of Thursday. The count rose through the day — about 47,000, then 50,000, then over 80,000 — and by Thursday afternoon Chief Secretary Anu Garg put it at 1,05,304 people in 1,224 cyclone and flood shelters, with evacuations still going on. Ganjam, under which Gopalpur lies, accounted for 45,364; Gajapati 18,000. The Special Relief Commissioner’s office said more than one lakh had been shifted, including close to 10,000 pregnant women.

Around it: 9,299 shelters set up, 305 emergency response teams deployed, and reservoirs in southern and coastal districts being emptied. Schools and Anganwadis are shut in six districts — Ganjam, Gajapati, Puri, Jagatsinghpur, Kendrapara and Kandhamal — from 19 to 21 November. Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi has said the state’s target is zero casualties, and it is aiming to evacuate two lakh people in total — so 1,05,304 is a midpoint, not a final total, and Thursday’s pace will have to continue through the night.

What 110 kmph, a metre of sea and 21 cm of rain actually mean

The wind is only one of three hazards. Sustained winds of 90–100 kmph, gusting to 110, can uproot trees, damage thatched and tin roofs and snap power lines. Squally winds of 40–50 kmph gusting to 60 were already prevailing along the south Odisha coast on Thursday.

The second hazard is the sea: a storm surge of 0.5 to 1 metre above the astronomical tide — enough to inundate low-lying areas near the shore, which is why the evacuation has concentrated on coastal villages in Ganjam and Puri. A metre of seawater moving across flat coastal land floods homes; it does not merely wet them.

The third hazard is rain, and it may last longest. The IMD warns of very heavy to extremely heavy rain — 21 cm and more in the heaviest falls — across Malkangiri, Koraput, Rayagada, Kalahandi, Gajapati, Ganjam, Kandhamal, Boudh, Puri, Nayagarh and Khurda on 19 and 20 November. Six districts — Kendrapara, Jagatsinghpur, Puri, Ganjam, Gajapati and Kandhamal — are under red warning for Thursday. Rain of that intensity means flooding and landslides in the interior even after the wind has passed.

Andhra Pradesh: spared the worst, not out of it

For Andhra Pradesh, Thursday’s news was better than a day earlier: as the track shifted toward Odisha, the impact lessened — but the state is not out of the storm. Three districts in north coastal Andhra Pradesh remain on red alert for extremely heavy rain, and the landfall corridor still begins at Narsapur. Fishermen have been warned off the sea. The difference between the two states on Friday will be one of degree — a direct landfall near Gopalpur versus the edge of the system at Narsapur — not of exposure.

What happens next

Three things will decide how Friday is remembered: whether the state reaches its two-lakh evacuation aim before noon; the landfall itself — point, hour, and whether winds arrive at the forecast 90–100 kmph or weaker; and the rain after landfall, which will decide whether the interior districts flood on Friday night and Saturday.

Until then, the position is as Thursday evening left it. A severe cyclonic storm is about 500 km south of Gopalpur and closing. Over a lakh are already in shelters, and the state wants twice that moved before Friday noon. The only number that will matter on Friday evening is the one the state has set as its target: zero casualties.

Quick Answers
When and where will Cyclone Akvan make landfall? +

By noon on Friday 20 November, between Narsapur in Andhra Pradesh and Puri in Odisha, close to east of Gopalpur, according to the IMD bulletin of 19 November. The corridor is Narsapur to Puri because the exact point could still shift slightly.

How strong will the winds be? +

Maximum sustained winds of 90–100 kmph, gusting to 110 kmph at landfall. The IMD classifies Akvan as a severe cyclonic storm — not a very severe one. A storm surge of 0.5–1 metre is also expected.

How many people has Odisha evacuated? +

1,05,304 people to 1,224 shelters by Thursday afternoon, per Chief Secretary Anu Garg — up from about 50,000 on Wednesday. Ganjam district alone accounts for 45,364. The state aims to evacuate two lakh people in total.

Is Andhra Pradesh safe? +

Mostly spared the worst as the track shifted toward Odisha, but not out of the storm: three north-coastal districts remain on red alert for extremely heavy rain, and the landfall corridor still begins at Narsapur.
