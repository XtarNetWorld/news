Three Nuclear Powers, One Room, No Deal Yet: What Trump, Xi and Putin Actually Agreed in Shenzhen

For the first time ever, the presidents of the United States, China and Russia sat down together — at a lakeside villa in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of APEC. Around 80 minutes later they left with no breakthrough on Ukraine, Iran or nuclear arms. The meeting itself was the news.

MS By Monu Sharma
• November 19, 2026 • 5 min read • World
01 First ever

The first trilateral meeting of the US, Chinese and Russian presidents, on Thursday 19 November 2026 at Kylin Villa, Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders’ Meeting.

02 Around 80 minutes

The talks ran about 80 minutes, including opening remarks to the media. Reports vary slightly — USA Today says 90 minutes, The Hindu around an hour and 20 minutes.

03 No breakthrough

No immediate breakthrough on Ukraine, Iran or nuclear arms was announced. Trump left straight for the airport.

04 The ICC moment

Trump called the International Criminal Court “out of control” in the room, with Putin — under an ICC arrest warrant since March 2023 — sitting across the table.

Never before have the leaders of the United States, China and Russia sat down together in one room. On Thursday, 19 November 2026, they finally did — Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin, around a round wooden table at the lakeside Kylin Villa in Shenzhen. “We are really making history,” Trump said. Around 80 minutes later, it was over. No deal on Ukraine, no deal on Iran, no deal on nuclear weapons. Trump headed straight for the airport. The history was the photograph — and the fact that, for the first time, the three men who control the world’s largest nuclear arsenals had talked face to face at all.

The day it happened

The trilateral was the last act of a day of carefully sequenced meetings. Xi and Putin had already met on Wednesday. On Thursday, Trump sat down with Putin for about 40 minutes — a meeting dominated by Ukraine, according to Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov, speaking via TASS — then held a bilateral with Xi, their third meeting of the year. Xi described US–China relations as “stable and improved”. Only then did the three come together, about an hour late, at the secluded government guesthouse at the foot of Shenzhen’s hills, a venue once visited by North Korea’s Kim Jong-il in 2006.

Trump opened by dismissing the diplomacy that came before it. “No problems were ever solved under the old school, refusing to talk about them,” he said — his argument being that talking to rivals is itself the achievement. Putin said all three shared an interest in resolving the war in Ukraine and a “responsibility” for global peace. When the meeting ended, he left without speaking to the media.

What was on the table

The agenda was the heaviest in world politics: nuclear arms control, the war in Ukraine, Iran, trade and technology, artificial intelligence, and the shape of the global order. Nuclear weapons came first. Trump framed the summit as primarily about reducing arsenals. Xi — whose military has been building up its nuclear arsenal — called for nuclear weapons to be abolished.

The urgency is real. New START, the last treaty limiting American and Russian strategic nuclear arsenals, expired in February 2026, leaving the world’s two largest nuclear powers without any binding limit for the first time in decades. US officials had played down any prospect of a breakthrough, calling the meeting a preliminary step. Trump had acknowledged agreement would be hard. On that, the day proved him right: no figure, no target, no timetable emerged.

The morning before the handshake

The war the three men were meeting to discuss arrived in Shenzhen before they did. Hours before the summit, Ukraine struck the bridge linking Russia and Crimea for the third time in a week, forcing a suspension of train services, Russian authorities said. The transport ministry said nobody was hurt and specialists were assessing damage to the 19-kilometre bridge, the flagship crossing Putin inaugurated in 2018. Russia, meanwhile, struck Ukraine as the talks went on — a blunt reminder of the gap between a summit photograph and a ceasefire.

The ICC moment

The most startling exchange had nothing to do with missiles. Trump told Xi and Putin the International Criminal Court was “out of control” and suggested the three powers cooperate against what he called “rogue globalist organisations”. It landed with force because of who sat across from him: in March 2023 the ICC issued an arrest warrant for Putin over the alleged unlawful deportation of Ukrainian children. The warrant remains in force.

Neither the US nor China is a party to the Rome Statute that established the court, and Russia does not recognise its jurisdiction. But for an American president to attack the court in the same room as its most prominent wanted man was extraordinary.

What was actually agreed — and what was not

Here is the honest accounting. A joint APEC statement released as the talks concluded was limited to pledges on trade, technology innovation and economic growth. That is the documented outcome. Chinese state media separately reported a “peace declaration” committing to “stable energy flows”. That wording — and whether it is an agreed outcome at all — has not been independently confirmed by Western outlets, and this article does not present it as agreed.

What is confirmed is the absence: no progress announced on Ukraine, Iran or nuclear arms. The White House had said no agreement was expected — and on that narrow point, the day delivered exactly what was promised.

Why the photo still matters

Comparisons are already being drawn to Yalta in 1945, when Roosevelt, Churchill and Stalin redrew post-war Europe. The comparison flatters Shenzhen and warns about it: Yalta produced decisions; Shenzhen produced a photograph and a conversation. But the photograph is not nothing — the leaders of the three largest nuclear powers have now sat in one room and talked about the weapons they control, in the year the last treaty limiting them lapsed.

The test is whether the conversation continues — whether officials negotiate a new arms-control framework, and whether the Ukraine discussion Trump and Putin held separately leads anywhere. Until then, the summary is short: three nuclear powers, one room, 80 minutes, no deal yet.
