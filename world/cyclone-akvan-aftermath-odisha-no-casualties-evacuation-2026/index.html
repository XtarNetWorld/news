The Storm That Weakened: How Odisha Evacuated 4.4 Lakh People and Lost No One to Cyclone Akvan

Forecast as a severe storm, Cyclone Akvan came ashore near Gopalpur on Friday weaker than expected — and the count that matters most is zero: no lives lost, after one of the largest evacuations Odisha has ever carried out.

Every cyclone is written about twice: once before landfall, when the fear is the forecast, and once after, when the counting begins. Cyclone Akvan’s second story is the opposite of its first. Forecast as a severe storm, it came ashore near Gopalpur on Friday morning weaker than expected — and the number that matters most from the weekend is not a wind speed or a rainfall total. It is zero: no lives lost, after one of the largest evacuations Odisha has ever carried out.

The storm that weakened

Cyclone Akvan made landfall near Gopalpur in Ganjam district on Friday, 20 November, with the landfall process running from about 8:00 to 11:20 in the morning. Sustained winds were 70–80 kmph, with gusts to 90 kmph. The forecast had warned of a severe storm, with winds of 90–100 kmph. What arrived was weaker than that — and by Friday evening, Akvan had weakened to a depression.

It was the third storm to threaten Odisha’s east coast in 24 days, and the third time the state had to prepare for the worst. That gap between the forecast and the reality is the first fact of the aftermath — and it is the reason the second fact, the evacuation, matters more than the damage count. A weaker storm is still a storm; the difference between a bad weekend and a disaster was decided before the wind arrived, in the hours spent moving people out of its path.

Zero: the number that matters

No lives were lost. That figure is consistent across every source that reported the aftermath — ABP Live, Down To Earth, New Indian Express, ETV Bharat and Outlook India all reported no loss of life and no major damage in Odisha. Odisha’s Revenue and Disaster Management Minister, Suresh Pujari, said “no major damage or loss of life had been reported.”

Zero is a strange number to lead a cyclone story with, because cyclone stories are usually led by what was destroyed. But zero is the figure that sits next to the evacuation numbers in every official briefing, and that pairing is the story officials themselves have stressed. A storm that was forecast to be severe arrived weaker — and the state had already moved hundreds of thousands of people out of the way before it did.

4.4 lakh people, moved before the wind

The evacuation count moved as official briefings updated through Friday and Saturday, so it has to be given as a range, not a single settled figure. Early reports put it at about 3 lakh people. Chief Secretary Anu Garg then put the figure at 3.72 lakh, with the district breakdown reported by Down To Earth: Ganjam 154,290; Kendrapada 51,956; Puri 40,295; Gajapati 28,693; and Khordha 23,061. The latest minister briefing, reported by New Indian Express on 21 November, put the total at 4,40,461 evacuated. The honest way to say it is this: over 4.4 lakh people were moved to safety, with the count rising from 3.7 lakh to 4.4 lakh as briefings updated.

Behind that number was a large operation. Officials said 9,341 shelters had been readied, with 3,653 operational, and 418–423 rescue teams from ODRAF, NDRF and the Fire Services were deployed. Fifty-six fishing boats and 498 fishermen were sheltered at port, and bus services were suspended in Ganjam. The scale is what makes the zero deaths figure believable: the evacuation was not a line in a press release, it was the main event of the weekend.

The births in the shelters

One quieter part of the operation was the movement of pregnant women to hospitals before the storm. Early briefings put that at 4,470 women moved, with 1,450 babies delivered safely. A later minister briefing updated those figures: 5,674 women shifted, and 2,316 babies delivered, officials said. As with the evacuation total, the later figures are the ones to use — and they should carry the attribution, because the count moved as briefings updated.

It is a small detail against the scale of a cyclone, but it says something about what an evacuation actually is. The shelters did not only hold people waiting out the wind; for a weekend, they doubled as maternity wards. That is the kind of fact that gets lost when a storm is reduced to wind speeds — and it is worth keeping, because it is the human side of the zero.

What the water left behind

The damage was real, even if it was limited. Heavy rain and flooding hit parts of Ganjam: 30 people were rescued from floodwaters in Kodala, and 8 people stranded in Berhampur’s Dura Kalinga Vihar were moved to safety. A landslide was reported near the Maa Taratarini Temple in Purushottampur. Power was disrupted in northern Odisha — in Bhadrak, Balasore and Mayurbhanj — with restoration underway.

Travel took the other hit. Forty-seven trains were cancelled, 41 of them by the East Coast Railway, and around 28–29 were rescheduled. None of this is minor for the people affected, and none of it should be minimised. But set against a forecast severe storm and an evacuation of over 4.4 lakh people, the aftermath reads as disruption and damage to be repaired — not as the disaster the forecast had warned of.

What happens next

The weather is not finished. The IMD has an orange alert in place for 14 districts, with 12–20 cm of rain expected, until Saturday 8:30 am, and rain is continuing in West Bengal till Monday. Power restoration in the north is underway, and the final official damage assessment has not yet been published.

What to watch now is the clean-up: restoration in Bhadrak, Balasore and Mayurbhanj, the final damage totals, and whether the rain in Bengal brings fresh flooding. But the story of this cyclone is already written, and it is not a story of destruction. A storm forecast as severe arrived weaker, a state moved 3.7–4.4 lakh people out of its path, and nobody died. That is the evacuation that worked — and it is the reason this aftermath reads so differently from the landfall story that came before it.

Did anyone die in Cyclone Akvan? +

No deaths were reported. ABP Live, Down To Earth, New Indian Express, ETV Bharat and Outlook India all reported no loss of life, and Odisha minister Suresh Pujari said no major damage or loss of life had been reported.
