Hurricane Polo Explodes to Category 5: Second-Strongest Pacific Storm on Record

In under 24 hours the system went from tropical storm to a 180 mph monster with 892 mb pressure. Live track, rainfall threats for Mexico, and the next 48 hours.

MS By Monu Sharma
• Sep 23, 2026 • 7 min read • World
01 Intensity

180 mph sustained winds · 892 mb pressure — second-lowest on record in the eastern Pacific.

02 Speed

Intensified from tropical storm to Category 5 in under 24 hours — one of the fastest rates ever observed.

03 Threat

3–8 inches of rain, life-threatening flooding & mudslides for coastal Guerrero and Michoacán through Thursday.

04 Track

Nearly stationary → slow west-northwest. Core expected to stay offshore but close enough to deliver heavy impacts.

Hurricane Polo has rewritten the eastern Pacific record books. What was a modest tropical storm with 50 mph winds on Monday morning became an extremely dangerous Category 5 hurricane by Tuesday afternoon — packing maximum sustained winds near 180 mph (285 km/h) and a central pressure of 892 millibars.

NOAA Hurricane Hunter aircraft confirmed the extreme intensity late Tuesday while the storm was nearly stationary roughly 215 miles (345 km) south of Zihuatanejo, Mexico. Polo now ranks as the second-most intense eastern Pacific hurricane on record by central pressure, trailing only the legendary Hurricane Patricia of 2015.

Why Hurricane Polo Exploded So Fast

The rate of intensification is among the most extreme ever measured in the basin. Winds increased by approximately 110–130 mph in a single 24-hour window — far beyond the official threshold for rapid intensification (35 knots / ~40 mph in 24 hours).

  • Ocean heat — Extremely warm El Niño waters exceeding 31 °C (88 °F) with deep warm-water pools that resist cooling from upwelling.
  • Low shear — Minimal vertical wind shear allowed the storm’s core to organize without disruption.
  • Moisture & structure — Abundant mid-level moisture and a compact, well-defined eye that efficiently converted heat into wind energy.

These conditions closely mirrored the environment that allowed Patricia to set records in 2015. Polo’s 892 mb pressure places it second only to Patricia’s 872 mb and ranks it among the strongest storms ever observed in the Western Hemisphere.

Current Position, Track & 48-Hour Outlook

As of the latest NHC advisory, Polo is centered near 14.6°N, 101.5°W. The storm is nearly stationary but is expected to begin a slow northwestward motion tonight, followed by a general west-northwestward track over the next several days.

The core is forecast to remain offshore of southwestern Mexico, but close enough to produce significant impacts along the coast through Thursday.

Hazards Affecting Land

Rainfall — 3 to 6 inches expected across coastal Guerrero and Michoacán through Thursday, with isolated totals up to 8 inches. This is capable of producing life-threatening flash flooding and mudslides, especially in areas of steep terrain.

Wind — Tropical storm conditions are expected within the warning area by early Wednesday and possible farther west into the watch area by Thursday. Hurricane-force winds remain well offshore.

Watches & Warnings

  • Tropical Storm Warning: Tecpan de Galeana to Punta San Telmo
  • Tropical Storm Watch: Westward to Playa Perula (Jalisco)

Polo is the third Category 5 hurricane of the 2026 eastern Pacific season and ties the early-season record for the earliest third Category 5 on record.

What to Expect in the Next 48 Hours

Wednesday through Thursday: Polo remains a powerful Category 5 hurricane while drifting slowly west-northwest parallel to the Mexican coast. The heaviest rainfall threat continues for Guerrero and Michoacán. Minor intensity fluctuations are possible due to eyewall replacement cycles, but the storm is expected to stay extremely dangerous.

Residents and travelers in the warning and watch areas should:

  • Monitor official NHC and Mexican meteorological updates every few hours
  • Avoid travel through flood-prone roads and mountain areas
  • Secure outdoor objects and prepare emergency kits
  • Follow local civil protection instructions regarding any evacuations

Quick Answers
How strong is Hurricane Polo right now? +

As of the latest National Hurricane Center advisory, Hurricane Polo is a Category 5 storm with maximum sustained winds near 180 mph (285 km/h) and a central pressure of 892 mb — the second-most intense eastern Pacific hurricane on record.

Will Hurricane Polo make landfall in Mexico? +

Current forecasts keep the core of Polo offshore of southwestern Mexico. However, the storm is close enough to produce tropical-storm-force winds, heavy rain of 3–8 inches, and life-threatening flooding and mudslides across coastal Guerrero and Michoacán through Thursday.

Why did Hurricane Polo intensify so rapidly? +

Polo intensified over extremely warm El Niño-fueled ocean waters, low wind shear, and abundant moisture. Winds increased by roughly 110–130 mph in 24 hours — one of the fastest rates of rapid intensification ever recorded in the eastern Pacific.
