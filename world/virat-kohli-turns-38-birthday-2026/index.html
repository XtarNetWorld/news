Virat Kohli Turns 38 on 5 November: A Birthday-Eve 74 at Eden Park

Virat Kohli celebrates his 38th birthday on Thursday 5 November 2026, in New Zealand, in the middle of a five-match ODI series — a day after a 74 off 51 balls carried India to a five-wicket win in the rain-hit first ODI at Eden Park, Auckland.

MS By Monu Sharma
• November 5, 2026 • 5 min read • World
01 Birthday

Born in Delhi on 5 November 1988; turns 38 on Thursday 5 November 2026, mid-series in New Zealand.

02 The innings

74 off 51 balls, 13 fours and a six, at a strike rate of 145.1, in India’s five-wicket DLS win on 4 November.

03 The record

86 international centuries — 30 Tests, 55 ODIs, 1 T20I — and his 80th ODI fifty on 4 November.

04 The milestone

First batter to reach 13,000 ODI runs at No. 3, taking his tally there to 13,062.

There are birthday gifts, and then there is the gift Virat Kohli gave himself on the eve of turning 38. On 4 November 2026, at Eden Park in Auckland, in a rain-hit first ODI against New Zealand, Kohli walked in early, after Rohit Sharma was dismissed for a six-ball duck, and scored 74 off 51 balls to carry India to a five-wicket win by the Duckworth-Lewis-Stern method. The crowd sang happy birthday a day early. Eden Park’s chief executive presented him with a cake. And on Thursday 5 November, Kohli turns 38 — not at home, not at a public event, but in New Zealand, in the middle of a five-match ODI series, with the next match already in view.

The birthday-eve innings

India were chasing a revised target of 143 under the Duckworth-Lewis-Stern method after repeated rain showers interrupted their reply at Eden Park on Wednesday 4 November. Kohli arrived in the second over, after Rohit Sharma’s early dismissal, and reached his half-century in 35 balls — his 80th ODI fifty — before finishing on 74 off 51 balls, with 13 fours and a six, at a strike rate of 145.1. India won by five wickets, taking a 1-0 lead in the five-match series.

The innings also carried a milestone that deserves its own sentence. Kohli became the first batter to reach 13,000 ODI runs while batting at No. 3, taking his tally at that position to 13,062 runs from 251 innings. He also moved level with Ricky Ponting for the most runs against New Zealand, with 1,971 runs from 37 matches. And in the field, he took a catch described in reports as a screamer, in a match where he was involved at every stage.

Former India head coach Ravi Shastri, in New Zealand on commentary duty, was among the first to wish Kohli publicly, praising the masterclass in India’s first ODI win. Fans in the stands at Eden Park held birthday messages and postcards, and the crowd celebrated Kohli a day early — a reminder that, for Indian fans travelling to New Zealand, a birthday in the middle of a series is still a birthday.

The cake, and why there is no public birthday event

Eden Park chief executive Nick Sautner presented Kohli with a special birthday cake after India’s five-wicket win on 4 November. Reports describe it as a birthday surprise ahead of Kohli’s 38th birthday, presented after his performance in the series opener.

What will not happen, on the evidence available, is a public birthday event. Kohli is mid-series in New Zealand, so any celebration on 5 November will be team-based, not a public event — and this article does not invent one. That distinction matters for fans: the birthday tributes, stats and fan edits that will dominate feeds on 5 November are the celebration, along with whatever the team does privately. The next on-field occasion is the 2nd ODI in Wellington on Saturday 7 November.

The numbers, in one place

Born in Delhi on 5 November 1988, Kohli made his ODI debut against Sri Lanka in August 2008. He has since scored 86 international centuries — 30 in Tests, 55 in ODIs and one in T20Is. The 55 one-day hundreds are the most in the format; he went past Sachin Tendulkar’s 49 with 117 against New Zealand in the 2023 World Cup semi-final.

Those numbers are why a 38th birthday still feels like a live sporting story rather than a nostalgia piece. Kohli is not being celebrated only for what he did. On 4 November, he did it again — a 74 off 51 balls, in a chase, in rain, in a series opener, a day before his birthday. Age, in this case, has not diminished influence in the 50-over format, and the birthday week is also a form guide for Wellington.
